J-1 Summer Work Travel

A summer working and travelling in the United States, on a J-1 visa.

Book a Consultation →

Summer Work Travel is administered by the U.S. Department of State’s Bureau of Educational and Cultural Affairs through authorised programme sponsors. You cannot join it directly: every placement runs through a sponsor the Department of State has designated.

Level
Post-secondary students enrolled in a full-time course outside the United States
Duration
A maximum of four months per season

Are You Eligible?

  • You are a post-secondary student, enrolled in and actively pursuing a degree or other full-time course of study at an accredited, classroom-based institution outside the United States.
  • You have successfully completed at least one semester or the equivalent of post-secondary study.
  • You have enough English to function effectively in an English-speaking environment.
  • You are pre-placed in a job before you travel — the Department of State requires this for applicants from countries outside the Visa Waiver Program, which includes Uzbekistan.
  • You return home before your own university term starts.

How to Apply.

  1. Step 1 Choose a sponsor designated by the U.S. Department of State, and confirm which one your placement runs through
  2. Step 2 Apply through that sponsor and be matched with a vetted job offer
  3. Step 3 Receive your DS-2019 form from the sponsor
  4. Step 4 Pay the SEVIS fee and apply for the J-1 visa at the U.S. Embassy
  5. Step 5 Travel, work the agreed job, and keep the monthly contact your sponsor requires
  6. Step 6 Return home before your university term begins

Fees and dates are set by each sponsor and by the U.S. Embassy, not by Study House. Confirm every amount in writing before you pay anything.

Questions Parents Ask.

Can Study House place me directly?

No. Only a sponsor designated by the U.S. Department of State can place you. Ask us which designated sponsor your placement would run through, and check that name on j1visa.state.gov before you pay anything.

What jobs are not allowed?

The Department of State excludes, among others: sales roles that require you to buy inventory, domestic help, clinical care with patient contact, adult entertainment, night work between 10pm and 6am, hazardous work, commission-only work with no guaranteed wage, and gaming or gambling roles.

Do I need a job before I fly?

Yes. Participants from countries outside the Visa Waiver Program must be pre-placed in a job before entry, and Uzbekistan is one of those countries.

Can I stay on after the summer?

No. The programme is a maximum of four months and you must return home before your own university term starts. Participants can be admitted to the programme more than once, in later seasons.
