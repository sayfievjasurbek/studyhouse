Scholarship routes
Chinese Government Scholarship + university awards

The Chinese Government Scholarship exempts application fee, tuition and dormitory fees, includes comprehensive medical insurance and pays undergraduates RMB 2,500 a month. USTC Fellowship (Level A) covers tuition, insurance, an RMB 2,500 monthly stipend and a housing subsidy. HIT, Fudan and the Beijing and Shanghai governments also offer awards.

Campus China · USTC · HIT

Language tests
Chinese (HSK) or an English test

Most undergraduate degrees are taught in Chinese: HIT asks HSK 4 (210+) and USTC asks HSK 5. English-taught undergraduate programs are limited — HIT: Civil and Chemical Engineering (IELTS above 6.0 or TOEFL above 80); Fudan: MBBS and Economics; BIT: IELTS 6.0 or TOEFL 85. USTC and HIT also require the China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA).

Visa path

X1 Student Visa. The Gateway to China.

Students who study in China for more than 180 days apply for an X1 visa with the admission letter and the study-visa application form issued by the university.

  1. 01Receive your admission letter and the Visa Application for Study in China (form JW201 or JW202)
  2. 02Gather your passport, photo and the documents your embassy asks for
  3. 03Apply for the X1 visa (study over 180 days) in person at a Chinese embassy or visa application center
  4. 04Pay the visa fee — it depends on your nationality, so check the embassy’s fee list
  5. 05Enter China and complete the health check your university requires
  6. 06Within 30 days of entry, apply for your residence permit at the local exit-entry administration

Questions parents ask

What families ask before choosing a Chinese university

Honest answers — including what Study House cannot promise.

Do I need to speak Chinese?

For most undergraduate degrees, yes. Chinese-taught programs ask for HSK 4 (HIT) or HSK 5 (USTC). English-taught undergraduate programs exist but are limited — for example Civil and Chemical Engineering at HIT, or MBBS and Economics at Fudan — and some need a minimum number of students to start.

What will studying in China really cost per year?

At the universities we could verify, tuition is roughly RMB 20,000–30,000 a year for regular undergraduate programs (more for medicine and special programs), plus about RMB 1,000–2,000 a month for living costs at Wuhan University’s estimate. The Chinese Government Scholarship can cover tuition and dormitory fees and pay a monthly allowance, but places are competitive.

Is it safe? What should we check?

Study House cannot give a safety rating for a city or campus. Check each shortlisted university’s student-support and safety information, follow your government’s travel advice, and ask us to help you compare cities on cost and student support.

What if the visa is refused?

Ask the embassy or visa center what was missing. In many cases you can apply again with corrected documents, and fees are generally not refunded. Your university’s international office can also confirm that your admission letter and JW form are correct. We can review the situation with you, but the embassy decides.

Do you guarantee admission or a visa?

No. Study House cannot guarantee admission or a visa. Universities decide admission and the government decides visas. What we do is help you choose realistic universities, prepare a complete application and avoid mistakes that can be avoided.
